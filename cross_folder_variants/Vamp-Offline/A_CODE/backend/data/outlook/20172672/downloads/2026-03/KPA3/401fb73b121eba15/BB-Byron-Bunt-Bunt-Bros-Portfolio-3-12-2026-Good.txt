Good day Hannes, From my perspective, I largely agree with Piet’s earlier point. Serious and educational games do not have the same market demand as AAA or even most indie entertainment titles, so pricing needs to be considered relative to the niche audience. On Prosper specifically, I agree with Byron that the price does feel high, although I do understand that some of the cost pressure likely comes from the specialised components, such as the transparent problem cards. For CiteSaga and Dogs of War, the pricing seems more reasonable when you consider the production realities. Dogs of War, for example, requires the metal container and specialised printing, while CiteSaga has a fairly large number of components and production steps. That being said, we could consider cheaper alternatives for thee games; as nothing in set in stone. On the digital side, controlled access with authenticated accounts could work well, especially if we think in terms of institutional or batch licences rather than individual consumer sales. I still feel the digital artefacts should be made OERs, but that is my own stance on the matter.
