# Authorship-Preserving Feedback in Postgraduate Writing

## Introduction

Postgraduate writers often need iterative feedback while developing disciplinary voice and academic identity. Structured formative feedback improves academic writing because it helps learners identify gaps between an intended argument and the evidence visible in a draft (Inouye & McAlpine, 2019). However, feedback systems can weaken learner agency when they silently replace the learner's own interpretive decisions.

This review argues that an authorship-preserving feedback process should diagnose claims, evidence, warrants, and limitations without producing submission-ready prose for the learner. Such a process necessarily produces stronger long-term academic identity than ordinary supervisor feedback (Boud & Molloy, 2013).

## Approach

The discussion uses a focused narrative review of scholarship on doctoral writing, formative feedback, learner agency, and AI-supported academic work. Sources were selected for conceptual relevance, but no formal database protocol, date range, or exclusion criteria were recorded. The analysis compares how each source frames feedback, revision ownership, and academic identity.

## Discussion

Inouye and McAlpine (2019) connect doctoral writing and feedback to the development of academic identity. This supports treating feedback as more than error correction. Boud and Molloy (2013) argue for feedback designs that build learners' capacity to judge their own work, which may provide a useful warrant for preserving revision ownership. Recent generative AI feedback research also reports changes in writing quality and revision behaviour among postgraduate language learners (Mekheimer, 2025).

The available literature proves that any AI writing assistant which preserves an audit trail will improve every postgraduate learner's writing. This conclusion is limited because the studies differ in setting, intervention, outcome measures, and the meaning assigned to learner agency.

## Conclusion

Authorship-preserving academic assistance should make source fit and revision decisions visible while leaving final prose and judgment with the learner. A stronger review would define authorship preservation operationally, document its search protocol, and distinguish evidence about writing quality from evidence about learner agency.
