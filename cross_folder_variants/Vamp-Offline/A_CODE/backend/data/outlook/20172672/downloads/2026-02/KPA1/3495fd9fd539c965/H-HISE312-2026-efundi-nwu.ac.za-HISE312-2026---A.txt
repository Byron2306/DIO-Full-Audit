PART A: Critique (High-Scoring Advice) Identify at least 12 clear flaws in Appendix A. Organise under the required headings: Introduction & Conclusion Teaching Strategy & Approach Assessment & Resources At least 4 flaws must address management of controversial/sensitive content. For higher rubric bands: Don’t just list problems — explain why they affect learning. Link flaws to History pedagogy (e.g. enquiry, multiperspectivity, evidence-based reasoning). Propose practical, realistic improvements. Superficial comments = Developing range. Insightful diagnosis + clear alternatives = Excellent range. PART B: Improved Lesson Plan To score well: Lesson aims must be measurable and skill-based (avoid “understand”). Show clear progression: recall → interpretation → source analysis → evidence-based comparison. Include a structured strategy for controversial content (e.g. ground rules, distancing techniques, structured discussion). Provide a short worksheet/source pack AND explain how it will be used. Assessment must align directly with your aims (formative + meaningful exit ticket). Teacher-centred lecture + opinion debate will not score highly. Evidence-based enquiry with scaffolding will. PART C: Reflection Strong reflections: Clearly identify what you changed most and why. Identify one real teaching risk. Explain how your redesign reduces that risk. Rubric Reminder Marks are weighted as follows: Teaching strategy & approach (10 marks) – this carries the most weight. Introduction & conclusion (8 marks) – clarity, cohesion, safe entry/closure. Assessment & resources (7 marks) – alignment and detail matter. The strongest submissions show: Clear alignment between aims, activities, and assessment. Professional handling of sensitive content. Purposeful use of sources and structured discussion. Evidence of critical engagement (not copied AI output). ⚖ If you use AI, include a short AI log and ensure you have critically edited the output.
