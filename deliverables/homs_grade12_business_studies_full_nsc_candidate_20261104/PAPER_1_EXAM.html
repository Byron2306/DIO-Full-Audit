Question 1: Case study — Khula Kitchens (40 marks)

Read the case study and answer all parts.
Khula Kitchens is a Gauteng food producer with 42 employees. A new municipal by-law requires grease traps and monthly inspections. The firm has received two written complaints about late orders. It plans to introduce a digital ordering system, train supervisors in quality control and renegotiate a supplier contract. Staff representatives have asked for a formal consultation before shift patterns change. Khula is also considering a second production line, but cash flow is tight.

1.1 Identify the relevant business environment for the municipal by-law and for the late-order problem. (4) [4]

1.2 Explain two ways the by-law can affect Khula’s operations. (8) [8]

1.3 Recommend and motivate two practical responses to the late-order complaints. (8) [8]

1.4 Discuss the value of consultation before changing shift patterns. (8) [8]

1.5 Advise whether Khula should add the second production line now. Use the case and two feasibility considerations. (12) [12]
